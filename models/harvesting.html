May harvesting¶

b_harvesting · tipbox.models.MayHarvesting

The biomass \(x\) follows logistic growth with saturating harvesting:

\[dx = \left[r x\left(1-\frac{x}{k}\right) - h(t)\frac{x^2}{s^2+x^2}\right]dt + \sigma\eta\,dW_t.\]

Here \(\eta\) is the noise argument. The initial harvesting rate is h_min. Over duration \(T\), it ramps to h_max by \(T/\text{param_end_multiplier}\) and then stays constant. The null ramp is capped at \(\max(h_{\min},2h_c-h_{\max})\).

For positive coefficients and \(s/k<1/\sqrt{27}\), the upper positive fold satisfies \(f(x,h)=0\) and \(\partial f/\partial x=0\):

\[x_c=\frac{k}{6}+\frac{k}{3}\cos\left[\frac13 \arccos\left(1-54(s/k)^2\right)\right],\qquad h_c=r\left(1-\frac{x_c}{k}\right)\frac{s^2+x_c^2}{x_c}.\]

critical_parameter computes this value unless h_bifurcation is explicitly set. Coefficients outside the analytic upper-fold regime raise a clear error when requesting an automatic marker. An explicit marker supports historical benchmark conventions.

Euler–Maruyama updates are projected to nonnegative biomass. Forced initial states use the configured uniform interval; null initial states use the clipped Gaussian controlled by null_initial_mean and null_initial_std.

The drift and default coefficients follow Bury et al. (2021). TipBox uses a capped null ramp and sampled initial states without burn-in; that paper uses fixed null harvesting h=0.15 and a 100-time-unit burn-in. These are different benchmark protocols. Supply constant forcing and a separate warm-up when reproducing that protocol.

Default critical parameter: 0.26043653. Automatic markers follow the physical coefficients where supported; AMOC uses an explicitly calibrated marker.
