Rosenzweig–MacArthur consumer-resource model¶

b_rosenzweig_macarthur_tc and b_rosenzweig_macarthur_hopf · tipbox.models.RosenzweigMacArthur

from tipbox.models import RosenzweigMacArthur

invasion = RosenzweigMacArthur(bifurcation_type="transcritical")
oscillation = RosenzweigMacArthur(bifurcation_type="hopf")

Resource \(x\) and consumer \(y\) obey

\[\begin{split}dx &= \left[r x(1-x/k)-\frac{a(t)xy}{1+a(t)hx}\right]dt +\sigma_1\eta\,dW_1,\\ dy &= \left[\frac{e a(t)xy}{1+a(t)hx}-my\right]dt +\sigma_2\eta\,dW_2.\end{split}\]

The attack rate uses the selected mode’s a_*_min and a_*_max. It ramps over \(T/\text{param_end_multiplier}\) and stays at its endpoint. The null cap defaults to \(\max(a_{\min},2a_c-a_{\max})\); a_*_null supplies an explicit alternative cap. Initial populations are sampled near the applicable initial equilibrium. Both population coordinates are projected to nonnegative values after each Euler–Maruyama step.

For positive \(k,e,h,m\) and \(e>mh\), the analytic thresholds are

\[a_{\rm TC}=\frac{m}{k(e-mh)},\qquad a_{\rm Hopf}=\frac{e+mh}{hk(e-mh)}.\]

These follow from the invasion eigenvalue and the coexistence-equilibrium Jacobian trace. Explicit a_*_bifurcation values override them. Invalid analytic regimes raise an error rather than reporting a spurious threshold.

TipBox uses capped null ramps and no burn-in. Bury et al. (2021) holds null attack rates at 2 (transcritical) or 12 (Hopf), uses a 100-time-unit burn-in, and ramps Hopf forcing to 16; TipBox’s Hopf endpoint is 17. The equations match, but the default simulation protocols differ.

Markers are continuous-system thresholds. At finite Euler–Maruyama step sizes, numerical oscillation onset can shift; refine dt when studying a Hopf onset.
